Digital Gardens vs. The Stream

Status: 🌿 Budding · Planted: May 1, 2026 · Last tended: June 10, 2026

For a long time, the dominant model of the web has been the reverse-chronological feed. Whether it's a social media timeline like Twitter or a traditional blog, the architecture prioritizes the absolute newest content. This is "The Stream." In the stream, context is fleeting. Old thoughts are buried beneath new ones, eventually vanishing into an unsearchable archive. You are always performing for the moment, feeding an insatiable algorithmic beast.

The digital garden presents an alternative. As Mike Caulfield originally articulated in his essay on the "Garden and the Stream," a garden is a topological space rather than a chronological one. It’s an evolving collection of knowledge where ideas are planted as rough notes and slowly cultivated over time.

In a garden, publishing is not a performative act of finalizing a perfect piece, but an ongoing process of learning. Notes interconnect through backlinks, forming a web of thought that mirrors the way human memory actually works. This architecture encourages slow, deliberate thinking and continuous revision. It values the depth and interconnectedness of ideas over the rapid-fire reaction of the hot take.

By moving away from the stream, we reclaim our time and our attention, building a personal body of knowledge that grows more valuable, rather than instantly obsolete, with each passing day.
